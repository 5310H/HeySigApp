# Automatic using the watch microphone

1. On the phone, use your usual waking program and turn Automatic Off before
   changing its microphone source. In **Watch connection**, select
   and enable your paired watch if it is not already selected.
2. Open **Noise → Choose programs**. Keep your desired Universal, Noisy and Very
   Noisy roles, switch on **Use watch microphone for Automatic**, and tap **Save
   speech program choices**.
3. Turn Automatic **On** on the phone and review the watch-input disclosure.
   The phone now waits for the selected watch; its microphone stays idle.
4. Within two minutes, open HeySig on the watch → **More → Automatic noise**.
   Tap **On**, review microphone use and grant permission/notifications if asked.
   If the phone has not been selected on the watch, use **More → Connect phone** first.
5. After both sessions start, either screen may sleep. Keep the phone where your
   hearing aids can hear its program-change tones.

The watch analyzes its own three-second microphone windows locally. Only compact
estimates and status go to the phone, which validates freshness, repeated agreement,
cooldown and your chosen programs before using its existing hearing-aid executor.
No audio or cleaned audio is streamed to the phone or aids. There is no enrollment,
noise-profile setup or learning period. Uncertain estimates hold the current program.

Use **Off** in either app or notification. Manual hearing-aid control, Therapy,
disconnection, removed permission/notification, stopping an app or eight-hour
expiry stops future automatic requests. Sessions never restart by themselves.
A remote microphone may take a short connection timeout to stop if the stop
message cannot be delivered; the phone's automatic authority closes locally.
Watch voice input and older foreground monitoring stop Watch Automatic before
using their microphone. Choosing phone microphone again requires a fresh explicit
phone session; the two sources do not run together.

The watch cannot identify the person you want to hear or measure speech
understanding. Wrist position, clothing, competing talkers, TV and microphone
gain can affect estimates. Watch accuracy, battery endurance and actual hearing-aid
reception must be recorded separately from enabling this feature. See the
[working checklist](watch-noise-completion-checklist.md).
