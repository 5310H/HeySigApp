# Hearing-aid programs in noise: clinical and technical review

Reviewed November 13, 2026. This follows the [manufacturer review](hearing-aid-concepts-2026-11-11.md)
and [phone listening/engineering evidence](../acceptance/phone-automatic-completion-2026-11-12.md).
“Materials” is interpreted as research and clinical documents; acoustic materials
and ear coupling are addressed separately below.

This review does not establish the owner's hearing-loss severity, exact aid
hardware/firmware, acoustic coupling or clinician-programmed settings. A loud
room and a poor speech-to-noise ratio are different problems. Proposed program
roles below are discussion briefs, not individualized prescriptions. No fitting,
levels, program assignments, detector thresholds or app code were changed.

## Proposed Noisy / Very Noisy program brief

This is our synthesis for discussion and testing, not settings to apply remotely.
There is no source-supported universal rule that “Very Noisy = all controls at maximum.”

| Saved role | Listening goal | What to ask the audiologist to configure or verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Universal | Everyday speech with useful environmental awareness | Verified speech audibility and comfortable output; document how its automatic microphone/noise features behave. Retain a reliable manual return program. |
| Noisy | A nearby conversation with competing sound | Appropriate directional/adaptive behavior for the actual speaker positions, speech-preserving noise management, and a demonstrable difference from Universal. A fixed forward focus should not be assumed suitable for side/rear speakers. |
| Very Noisy | More difficult speech competition or reverberation | Evaluate the strongest *useful* supported speech-focus strategy while checking words understood, comfort and awareness. If benefit remains inadequate, assess a compatible remote microphone near the wanted speaker; verify its mix with local aid microphones. |

The microphone/feature discussion follows NICE and the 2026 guidelines above;
the audibility and input-mix checks follow ASHA and the 2024 verification chapter.
The remote-input option is supported by the 2021 study, not a promise of benefit.
A remote microphone is especially relevant to one identified speaker; a group
conversation needs its own positioning/turn-taking assessment.

Do not infer gain, maximum output, compression ratio/speed, frequency lowering,
or noise-reduction strength from a program name or phone noise estimate. These
must be individualized and verified. The 2026 technical review explains why
simply making sound louder or changing compression more aggressively can have
unwanted consequences; it does not justify one fixed fast/slow setting for everyone.

## Physical materials, ear coupling and room sound

Open/closed fitting evidence does not justify swapping to a sealed dome by trial
and error. Ask whether the existing dome/vent/earmold limits the benefit of the
programs, and have any fitting change verified for audibility, feedback and own
voice. Acoustic fit and processing work together, with trade-offs.

Room treatment is different from electronic cancellation. Absorptive surfaces
can reduce reverberation; reducing competing sources and speaker distance also
changes the listening problem. [ASHA's acoustics guidance](https://www.asha.org/Practice-Portal/professional-issues/classroom-acoustics/)
explains these mechanisms, although its classroom targets should not be copied
as adult home-program specifications. This is not a recommendation to buy a
particular panel, foam, dome material or hearing aid.

Difficult speech listening and hazardous sound exposure must remain separate.
Noise-reduction settings or HeySig's score do not establish exposure safety.
Use an appropriate hearing-conservation/protection plan for hazardous noise;
[NIOSH protection guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/noise/prevent/ppe.html)
addresses that separate problem. HeySig is not a calibrated exposure meter.

## Information to request about the existing programs

A useful written fitting summary would identify:

- Exact aid model, technology level, firmware and current dome/earmold/vent.
- Universal, Noisy and Very Noisy slot numbers and their intended situations.
- Which microphone strategy, speech enhancement, steady-noise reduction, wind/
  transient processing, gain/output and compression settings differ between them.
- Any saved volume/balance/tinnitus targets applied when switching.
- Existing real-ear/feature-verification results, speech-in-noise findings and
  any remote-input compatibility/mix configuration.

This is a request for actual existing settings and clinical judgment. It does
not presume an appointment, request changes to a fitting or authorize contacting
anyone. Do not populate unknown values from a manufacturer's brochure.

## HeySig implications and evaluation

The existing validated HeySig controls select saved programs and documented user
levels. No fitting-DSP interface for changing microphone patterns, denoising,
compression or maximum output has been verified. Selecting Noisy does not create
those settings; they must already exist in the hearing-aid program.

These are engineering inferences, not clinical recommendations from the papers:

1. Preserve saved role assignments and one validated executor. Program content
   may be documented with provenance, but unknown details must not create an
   enrollment/calibration/acceptance enable lock.
2. Keep the scene estimate separate from wanted-speaker identity, ear-level SNR,
   actual aid reception and wearer benefit. Phone/wrist evidence does not measure
   the sound at the aided ears. A clean-looking RNNoise estimate is not a word score.
3. Continue the implemented manual A/B/return comparison. For stronger evidence,
   vary order, use comparable speech material and evaluate one configuration change
   at a time with clinical support. Clinical speech-in-noise assessment requires
   suitable validated, calibrated methods; an informal app trial is not equivalent.
4. Record understanding, effort, comfort and awareness separately. The owner's
   November 12 TV/nearby-person trial found understanding about the same and other
   outcomes uncertain. Do not overwrite that result with a presumed benefit.
5. Add no automatic “stronger processing” rule from these papers alone. First
   obtain the program summary, then evaluate held-out TV, competing-speaker,
   wind/rub, quiet-return and placement cases. Preserve confirmations, cooldown,
   manual/Therapy priority, privacy bounds and fitting/settings.
